Master’s Thesis

Applied research in deep learning, computer vision and ensemble learning, conducted in collaboration with Nokia to investigate the robustness and generalisation of industrial image classification systems.

Thesis Overview

Master’s in Collective Intelligence · 2023–2024 · Nokia

This thesis investigated how advanced deep learning architectures and ensemble learning techniques could enhance the performance and robustness of industrial image classification systems.

The research was conducted as part of an industry-embedded internship at Nokia Morocco and focused on the classification of industrial images under real-world constraints including class imbalance, limited labelled data, model overfitting and computational cost.

Research Questions

  • How can advanced CNN architectures enhance generalisation in industrial image classification tasks?
  • To what extent can ensemble learning reduce the effects of overfitting and class imbalance in real-world datasets?
  • How do individual deep learning architectures compare when evaluated under industrial data constraints?

Methodology

  • Comparative evaluation of CNN architectures including NASNetLarge, InceptionV3, DenseNet and EfficientNet
  • Two dataset structuring approaches to evaluate cross-domain generalisation
  • Data preprocessing and augmentation to reduce overfitting and increase training diversity
  • Feature extraction using YOLOv5
  • Ensemble learning through prediction aggregation
  • Evaluation using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and confusion matrices
  • Error analysis to investigate model behaviour beyond overall classification accuracy

Key Results

  • Best-performing individual architecture: DenseNet169
  • Best individual model accuracy: 62.37%
  • Ensemble model accuracy: 69%
  • Ensemble learning produced stronger overall generalisation than the best individual model
  • Model evaluation highlighted the impact of dataset imbalance and limited examples across industrial checkpoints

Technical Challenges

  • Significant imbalance between Pass and Fail samples
  • Limited labelled training data for certain industrial checkpoints
  • Risk of overfitting when training high-capacity CNN architectures
  • High computational requirements associated with large deep learning architectures
  • Differences in image characteristics across industrial inspection environments

Limitations & Future Work

Further work could investigate cost-sensitive learning, enhanced resampling techniques, transfer learning and larger labelled datasets to address class imbalance and improve model generalisation.

Human-in-the-loop approaches could also be explored to combine automated feature extraction with expert feedback in cases where model confidence is low or training data remains limited.
